# OptiSched: Faculty Commercialization Advisor

---

## Page 1: What it is

OptiSched is a course-scheduling tool for a department. The scheduling is done by a constraint solver, not by an AI model. We added a **Faculty Commercialization Advisor** for faculty who want to commercialize research.

**How it works (4 steps)**

1. **Drop in a paper (PDF).** It drafts the 26 fields of the UM-16C invention disclosure. Every filled value shows the quote from your paper it came from. Fields it cannot support, such as signatures and inventorship, stay blank.
2. **Refine.** It asks a few targeted questions (public disclosure, funding, student contributors, outside interests). It then builds a to-do checklist, each item citing the policy passage behind it. You can also ask a policy question and get word-for-word quotes from 425 passages across 7 University of Missouri and UMKC sources. If nothing relevant exists, it says so.
3. **Validate.** It suggests possible industries (NAICS codes, SBIR/STTR agencies) as hypotheses. A lookup of related papers and federal grants uses only broad terms you can edit first. By default it shows saved results, labelled as saved.
4. **Export.** A zip with the draft form, checklist, market notes and a one-page non-confidential summary, plus a pre-filled email to the technology office. Nothing is stored on our server.

**Course-release check:** if you need a course release, the checklist can run the real scheduling solver and show which course moves and who covers it. It does not estimate cost.

## What it does not do

- It gives no patentability, novelty or legal opinion.
- It has no patent search.
- It does not submit anything. There is no portal integration.
- It does not decide inventorship.

## Honest status

- Wording of the form fields, checklist and sources has **not been reviewed by the technology office**.
- It makes no claims about time saved. We have no measurements.
